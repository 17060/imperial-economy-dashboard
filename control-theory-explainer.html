ANALYTICAL ANNEX · 2024 DATA

The dashboard is satire. The axes are real control levers.

The Imperial Economy Dashboard is a fictional Sith command console. Strip the costume and four of its pillars map onto textbook geopolitical control theory: state and non-state actors observe a system, exert pressure through narrow leverage points, measure feedback, and adjust. This page reframes each axis as a mechanism and anchors it to published 2024-era data.

Energy Chokepoints Debt Leverage Fragmented Governance AI Surveillance Readiness

Framing note: this is descriptive analysis of structural levers visible in public reporting. It is not operational advice and does not endorse any coercive use of the mechanisms it describes.

The control loop, generalised

Each axis below is one instantiation of the same feedback loop: a sensor reads system state, a controller compares it to a desired set-point, and an actuator applies pressure through a chokepoint. The four axes differ only in which physical or institutional layer the chokepoint sits on.

The four axes below correspond to four chokepoint classes: physical flows (energy), capital flows (debt), institutional cohesion (governance), and information/identity infrastructure (AI surveillance).

AXIS 01 · PHYSICAL FLOWS

Energy Chokepoints

Satirical framing → "Sever seven hydrocarbon arteries to induce capitulation."
Control-theory mechanism → Maritime chokepoints concentrate global petroleum and LNG flows through a small number of narrow straits, so localised conflict or insurance risk becomes a global price and logistics actuator with measurable, fast feedback.

Diverted crude and oil-product traffic adds roughly two weeks of sailing time per voyage, tightening tanker capacity and pushing insurance premia upward.1

Strait of Hormuz · Persistent
20.9 million b/d
2023 average — ~20% of global petroleum liquids consumption

About one-fifth of global LNG trade also transited Hormuz in 2023. No comparable rerouting option exists, which is what makes it the canonical example of an inelastic physical chokepoint.1

Loop signature
  • Sensor: AIS shipping data, tanker tracking, ICE Brent futures.
  • Actuator: drone/missile attacks, port closures, insurance war-risk listings.
  • Feedback: spot freight rates, refinery margins, retail fuel prices within weeks.
  • Gain: high — small physical disruptions produce outsized price moves.

Source: U.S. EIA, "Fewer tankers transit the Red Sea in 2024" (Today in Energy).

AXIS 02 · CAPITAL FLOWS

Debt Leverage

Satirical framing → "Credit is obedience written in future tense."
Control-theory mechanism → Refinancing dependence on a handful of reserve currencies and bilateral creditors converts ordinary credit access into a slow-acting but durable governance lever. The actuator is the rollover decision, not the headline interest rate.

Loop signature
  • Sensor: sovereign yield spreads, IMF Article IV reports, CDS pricing.
  • Actuator: rollover terms, conditionality, currency-swap access, secondary-market sanctions.
  • Feedback: reserve drawdowns, exchange-rate moves, fiscal contraction over quarters.
  • Gain: moderate but persistent — the lever accumulates compound effects over years.

Source: World Bank, "Developing countries paid record $1.4 trillion on foreign debt in 2023" — International Debt Report 2024.

AXIS 03 · INSTITUTIONAL COHESION

Fragmented Governance

Satirical framing → "Weak spots in planetary command."
Control-theory mechanism → Fragmentation cuts both ways. It is a vulnerability for coordinated defence — coalitions cannot align — and a friction layer against any single actor's attempt at centralised control. In control terms, it raises the noise floor on every other axis.

Sahel coup belt · since 2020

Six Sahel-region countries experienced coups between 2020 and 2024, a concentrated cluster of institutional rupture that has reshaped regional security partnerships.3

Loop signature
  • Sensor: election observations, judicial independence indices, civic-space monitors.
  • Actuator: diplomatic recognition, multilateral votes, security partnerships, sanctions packages.
  • Feedback: measured in years; coalition stability and treaty adherence.
  • Gain: low per-step, high cumulative — fragmentation compounds across the other three axes.

Source: Freedom House, Freedom in the World 2024 (digital booklet, PDF).

AXIS 04 · INFORMATION & IDENTITY

AI Surveillance Readiness

Satirical framing → "Nascent control tools."
Control-theory mechanism → Compute, models, datasets, and the regulatory perimeter around them are becoming strategic governance infrastructure. Readiness is a composite of model capability, digital identity / data access, censorship capacity, and biometric deployment.

Model capability · Stanford AI Index 2024

U.S.-based institutions produced 61 notable AI models in 2023, vs 21 from EU institutions and 15 from China. Generative-AI private investment reached $25.2B in 2023, roughly 8× 2022.4

Regulatory perimeter

U.S. AI-related regulations grew from 1 in 2016 to 25 in 2023, a 56.3% year-over-year increase from 2022 — a fast-moving sensor on what the controller is willing to touch.4

Internet-freedom deployment surface

Internet freedom declined for the 14th consecutive year; 27 of 72 countries tracked saw declines, 18 improved. Arrests for online expression occurred in at least 56 of 72 countries. Freedom House notes a rise in AI-driven biometric surveillance, commercial spyware without oversight, and data localisation requirements facilitating state access.5

Loop signature
  • Sensor: network telemetry, biometric capture, ad-tech and platform logs.
  • Actuator: takedowns, throttling, identity-linked access controls, targeted spyware.
  • Feedback: near-real-time engagement and movement data; second-order political effects over months.
  • Gain: rising — compute and model quality compound the actuator's reach faster than oversight adapts.

Sources: Stanford HAI, 2024 AI Index Report · Freedom House, Freedom on the Net 2024 (digital booklet, PDF).

Synthesis · why these four together

No single axis explains modern coercive statecraft. Energy gives fast actuators with narrow geographic surface area; debt gives slow actuators with wide institutional surface area; governance fragmentation determines whether targets can mount a coordinated response; AI-surveillance readiness determines how cheaply the controller can read state and pre-empt feedback. The Imperial dashboard's joke is the dark theme. The non-joke is that all four loops are visibly closing in published 2024 data.
